**Reading.**

- naive recalled 13 of 13 facts in both runs and was the cheapest and fastest arm: over 32 turns the full history is short, append-only and mostly cached.
- topk recalled 10 and 8 of 13: per-query selection left out blocks the probes needed. extract recalled 12 or 13.
- Every extract miss is the same probe (turn 26): the talk length went from 30 to 25 and back to 30; the extractor can only append, judged 30 already present, and the block ended with the stale 25 as its latest line.
- H1 (window epoch 3) sent more prompt tokens without a higher cached share: no measured gain.
- H2 (extraction every two turns) gave the two lowest cold path costs of the extract variants with the same recall; two runs per arm do not settle it.

**Limits.**

- Two runs per arm, one synthetic French scenario of 32 turns, one model; recall is a substring check of 13 planted facts, not overall answer quality.
- Extraction calls are priced with their cached tokens (fixed on 2026-10-24); earlier figures price them as uncached.
- Short session: naive's history never grew large here, so this says nothing about long sessions.

# H5: the extractor may replace a corrected fact

Latency statistics skip the first turn of each run. Reproduce: `as memory-recall-hypotheses, with -e HC_EXTRACT_REPLACE=on [-e HC_EXTRACT_EVERY=2]`

**Reading.**

- With replace on, extract recalled 13 of 13 facts in all four runs, including the corrected talk length that plain extract missed once in two runs.
- The extractor replaced 4 to 6 lines per run and never named a line it could not find.
- Replace with extraction every two turns had the lowest total cost of every harness-cache run on this scenario (0.0086 and 0.0090 USD), with full recall.

**Limits.**

- Two runs per arm, one scenario with one corrected fact; a substring check of 13 facts.
- The baseline extract runs are the two of memory-recall-hypotheses, recorded the same day on the same model.
