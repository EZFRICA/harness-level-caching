# The cluster under load and under faults

Generated by `harness-cache figures` from `docs/measured/runs/`. Do not edit.

**Question.** Do three workers keep answering when one is killed or when the shared tier stops, and what is lost?

| `cluster-s5-kill-w2-v2` | 3 | 9 | kill -9 of w2, client without connect timeout | 216/216 | 45 | 4 | 1.97 | 2373 | 6536 | 40359 | 47.1 | 0 |
| `cluster-s5-kill-w2-v3` | 3 | 9 | kill -9 of w2 after 25 s | 216/216 | 24 | 4 | 2.94 | 2363 | 5399 | 11081 | 10.1 | 0 |

Latencies are client side, per request, every request included. Reproduce: `./scripts/cluster_fresh.sh <id> && docker compose exec -T hc harness-cache load --workers http://hc-w1:8797,http://hc-w2:8797,http://hc-w3:8797 --agents 9 --turns 24 --concurrency 9 --run-id <id>; then docker kill -s KILL hc-w2 or docker stop hc-postgres; then uv run python scripts/fault_facts.py`

**Reading.**

- Every request was answered in every run, through a worker kill and a 30 s outage of the shared tier.
- After a kill, an agent comes back on another worker in about 10 s: the 6 s lease plus the client's retry schedule. Without a short connect timeout the client waited for a silent dead worker and the worst request took 45 s (v2).
- Nothing was lost at the kill: replication runs every second, not only when the worker is idle. Before that change, the first kill lost 26 journal rows per agent (ledger, fault event).
- During the outage turns went on locally and the journal caught up when Postgres came back.

**Limits.**

- Echo model with a latency model fitted on real calls (base about 1 s, weak fit); 9 agents of 24 turns; one run per fault.
- Worker limits 1 CPU and 1 GB each, all on one machine: network faults between machines are not exercised.
- RPO is read from the killed worker's volume after the run; RTO from the client's first answer with a higher epoch.
